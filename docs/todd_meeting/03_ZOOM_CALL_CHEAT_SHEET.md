# Todd Zoom / FaceTime Call Cheat Sheet

## A. Opening (30–60 seconds)

“Todd, it’s great to reconnect. Your original note stuck with me because the
work you described—building funeral-home and association lists, marketing the
right offer, and following up properly—was exactly the kind of problem I wanted
to solve well. I’ve since built a working funeral-industry research and outreach
system. I’d like to understand which part matters most to you now, show you how
the work connects to it, and see whether a small paid test makes sense.”

## B. Transition from Todd's original email

“The project grew directly out of the practical work in your original email. I
realized a contact list alone becomes stale and has to be rebuilt for every
campaign. So I built a reusable research base that consolidates organizations,
checks public contact details, shows why each prospect fits, prioritizes the
list, and keeps outreach and follow-up from becoming disconnected spreadsheets.”

## C. 90-second platform explanation

“In plain English, I now have a structured Canadian funeral-home research base.
It brings duplicate listings together, checks public websites and business
contact details, captures relevant services and named roles where the evidence
supports them, and helps rank the organizations that best fit a particular
offer. For each prospect, I can explain why it was selected and prepare a
relevant approach based on public evidence rather than generic mail merge copy.

“The workflow also tracks whether an organization has been researched, reviewed,
contacted, replied, moved to a meeting, or reached a proposal. Final approval and
sending remain human-controlled, and manually sent messages can be recorded so
we do not contact the same organization twice by accident. The useful part for
you is that the same foundation can be segmented for grief seminars, convention
presentations, or celebrant training instead of starting over each time.”

## D. Ask Todd this early

> “Before I get too far into what I've been building, which part of the proposals
> interested you most?”

## E. Best discovery question

> “Of everything you originally wanted me to help with, which one would make you
> money fastest if I could get it working consistently?”

## F. Discovery questions

- Which offer is the priority right now: grief seminars, presentations,
  celebrant training, or something else?
- Who is the ideal buyer or decision-maker for that offer?
- Which geography should come first, and are there regions to exclude?
- What brochures, emails, presentations, testimonials or landing pages already
  exist?
- What outreach have you tried, and what happened?
- Do you have existing lists or relationships that must be excluded or merged?
- What follow-up cadence feels appropriate, and who should handle replies?
- If interest arrives, how many conversations or bookings can you handle?
- What outcome would make a 30-day test worthwhile to you?
- Do you prefer a defined pilot, hourly contractor support, or a mix of both?

## G. Pilot pitch

“I’d suggest we begin with the grief-information seminars and a deliberately
small Canadian segment. Over 30 days, I would help define the target, research
and qualify roughly 25 to 50 organizations, validate the available public
contacts and selection evidence, align the message with you, track the initial
approach and follow-up, and give you a clear results summary. The fixed price is
CAD $900. It is a bounded test of the offer and process, not a promise of a
particular response rate.”

## H. Pricing answers

Keep the commercial boundary clear: Alex independently built and retains the
reusable platform; Todd's new payment covers the agreed pilot and Todd-specific
deliverables going forward; work after or outside that scope starts at CAD
$35/hour, with priorities and hours agreed in advance.

**“Okay, how does payment work?”**

“For the 30-day pilot, I'd keep it simple: $450 to activate the project and
$450 when the pilot is complete. That way we're both committing to a defined
piece of work rather than leaving it open-ended.”

**“What are you thinking hourly?”**

“For ongoing contractor work, I was thinking around $35 an hour. For the
marketing side, I'd rather define a small fixed-budget pilot first so we both
know exactly what we're testing.”

**“Why $900?”**

“It covers a complete bounded test: target definition, prospect research,
contact and evidence review, message alignment, tracking, follow-up, and a final
recommendation. It gives us a meaningful result without committing either of us
to a large campaign.”

**“Can we start smaller?”**

“Yes. We could do a 10-hour validation for CAD $350. I’d keep the scope tighter:
define the offer, produce a smaller reviewed sample, and recommend the next
step.”

**“Can you just give me the list?”**

“I can provide a campaign-specific list as a deliverable, but the value is in
qualification, current evidence, contact review, and follow-up discipline. A raw
list alone goes stale quickly and does not tell us who is appropriate for this
offer.”

**“How many funeral homes do you have?”**

“The current Canadian dataset has 211 consolidated organizations across all 10
provinces. That is a research base, not 211 automatically contactable leads. We
would select and review a much smaller segment for your offer.”

**“Are the emails verified?”**

“Public email information is checked for format, source and mail-domain evidence.
That does not prove a particular mailbox is active, deliverable, or consenting,
so every campaign still needs review.”

**“Can this send automatically?”**

“It can prepare and track outreach, but the current process keeps final approval
and sending human-controlled. That protects quality and avoids accidental or
duplicate contact.”

**“Do you guarantee responses?”**

“No. I can improve targeting, evidence quality and follow-up discipline, but I
would not promise responses or sales. The pilot is designed to give us real
evidence about the offer.”

**“Who owns the software?”**

“I built the underlying reusable platform independently and retain it; that
prior development is not a debt or part of the pilot fee. For the new paid
engagement, you receive the Todd-specific campaign lists, reports and
deliverables we agree on, and you keep ownership of your brand, content and
business data. Work after or outside the defined pilot can start at CAD $35 an
hour, with priorities and hours agreed in advance.”

**“Can Life Celebrants use the system too?”**

“Yes. We can use the same research foundation for a separate celebrant-training
campaign, with its own audience, message and tracking. I would validate one offer
first, then reuse what we learn.”

**“Can we target the US?”**

“Yes, as a separate expansion. The platform supports controlled US association
discovery, but I would first define the target and validate US coverage and
campaign requirements before quoting volume or timing.”

**“How quickly can we start?”**

“Once we agree on the offer, scope and start date—and I have your current
materials—and the activation payment is confirmed, I can begin with target
definition and selection. I’ll confirm the working schedule in the written
follow-up.”

## I. Closes

**1. Todd is enthusiastic**

“Great. Let’s confirm the grief-seminar pilot at CAD $900: $450 to activate the
project and $450 when the pilot is complete. If you send me the current seminar
materials and confirm the activation payment by [DATE], I’ll return the one-page
target brief by [DATE] and we’ll use that as the kickoff.”

**2. Todd is interested but unsure**

“It sounds as though the opportunity is interesting, but the first test still
needs sharpening. How about I send a one-page recap with the recommended target,
scope and decision points, and we book a 20-minute decision call for [DATE]?”

**3. Todd wants something smaller**

“Let’s use the 10-hour, CAD $350 validation. We’ll define one offer, build and
review a smaller sample, and finish with a clear recommendation. The CAD $350 is
payable when we approve the scope so I can begin. If it looks sound, we can
carry the learning—not promise the result—into a later campaign.”

## J. Things not to say

- Do not promise leads, responses, conversions, revenue, compliance or results.
- Do not call DNS evidence proof that a mailbox is active or deliverable.
- Do not describe public contact information as consent.
- Do not say the system “scrapes everyone and emails them automatically.”
- Do not describe a bounded scan as proof that a feature or service is absent.
- Do not casually give away ownership of the platform.
- Do not overwhelm Todd with implementation details, internal scores or IDs.
- Do not claim Todd commissioned or owns all prior development.
- Do not frame past development time as a debt Todd owes.
